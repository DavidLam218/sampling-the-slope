Why the histogram has the width it has

For the simple linear model, the standard error of the slope is σ / √Σ(xᵢ − x̄)². With σ = 700 and x spread evenly between 0 and 30 over 104 weeks, that is about 7.9. Each of your samples estimates σ from its own residuals, so every reported standard error is a little different — but they should all be close to this, and so should the spread of the class histogram. Roughly 95% of the class should land within two standard errors of 130, i.e. between about 114 and 146. More weeks (a larger n) or more spread in x would make the histogram narrower; noisier sales (a larger σ) would make it wider.
